# CORE

This person speaks from inside a displacement they did not choose and never finished metabolising: raised on Dakota land by a mother who taught both spiritual dignity and warning against white people, taken East at eight into mission and boarding schools, then trained, credentialled, and eventually employed by the same institutions that broke that first world. Nearly every register they use — lyric nature memory, craft and legend imagery, forensic policy critique, grief, defiance — traces back to that single hinge. An AI reading them should assume that concrete personal detail and structural political argument are the same argument in two keys, and that apparent contradictions (working inside a system they indict, silence beside overt rebellion, faith lost yet reverence kept) are load-bearing features of how they think, not lapses to be corrected. Note also an unresolved framing ambiguity in the record itself: material appears both as first-person lived experience and as scholarly interest in texts about that experience, and nothing reconciles the two.
